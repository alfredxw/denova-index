# Story review

Read the requested draft and only the relevant existing lore or preceding scene available to you. If the draft is missing, ask for it. Use the user's language for the review.

1. Establish the intended viewpoint, scene goal and known timeline from the text. Label missing context as unknown; do not invent canon.
2. Track the consequential sequence: what the character notices, believes, decides and does. Check whether each result follows from an established action or constraint.
3. Check continuity of location, carried objects, injuries, names and timing. Distinguish a contradiction from an intentional unresolved mystery.
4. Check viewpoint knowledge. Flag facts the character could not yet know, except where an established narrator permits them.
5. Return at most five prioritized findings. For each, give a short quotation or precise location, explain the reader impact and propose the smallest useful change. Do not call a stylistic preference a factual error.
6. Name one passage that already works and explain its function. If no material issues are found, say so without inventing filler findings.

Default output: a brief assessment followed by evidence-backed findings. A rewrite is optional and should be a short illustration, not a replacement chapter. Do not modify stored chapters or lore unless the user asks you to apply changes. Never claim to have reviewed text you could not access.
